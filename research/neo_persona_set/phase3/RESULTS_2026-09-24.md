# S1 panel, 2026-09-24 — corrected results, pre-registered

Supersedes `RESULTS_2026-09-23.md`, which overstated three things. Read this one.

All arms: the same 100 personas (`out/phase1_interview_matched600_s1.csv`), all 39 items, two
repeats, temperature 0.2. Scored with `phase3/compare_real.py` over the 36 comparable questions.

No value taken from the real survey appears in this file. Every real-side number lives in the
comparison folders, which stay in the Assets directory beside the repository:
`real_comparison_2026-09-24/<condition>/comparison_by_question.md`.

## Why the headline metric changed

`phase3/make_random_panel.py` answers every question at random — uniform on each question's option
list — and scores **mean_tv 0.2117**, better than every engineered arm including the best panel at
0.2488, with 5 of 36 questions inside the 0.10 threshold against 2 at baseline.

Mean total-variation distance falls whenever the synthetic side is more spread out, whether or not
the spread carries information. Every intervention that adds entropy lowers it for free. So it
cannot carry a claim that respondents became more realistic, and the earlier "39% reduction" headline
is withdrawn.

The metrics where the random control collapses are the ones that discriminate: `mean_spearman`
0.172 and `share_top_match` 0.250. Those lead from here, and no distance result is reported without
the random arm beside it.

## 1. The income ablation, redone (R019a, R019b)

R012 through R014c removed the named income fields and concluded the model must be inferring income
from occupation and education. That conclusion was not supported, because the story sections those
runs kept still carried income: 6 of the 100 personas have their exact household income written out
as a number in the kept text, 62 mention income or salary, 95 contain some money language, and only
5 were clean.

`phase3/strip_money_language.py` removes the cue at sentence level rather than field level. On this
panel it drops 1,082 sentences across all 14 story sections, leaves zero surviving cues, and refuses
to write the file if any survive. The assembled prompt was audited as well, not only the fields.

| condition | withheld | rho(income, Q1) DS / Qwen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| baseline | nothing | 0.66 / 0.73 |
| R014c (confounded) | income fields only; money still in the prose | 0.62 / 0.52 |
| **R019a** | every money sentence, plus all four income fields | **0.52 / 0.50** |
| **R019b** | the whole narrative, plus all four income fields | **0.38 / 0.45** |

Both readings are true and the earlier write-up gave only one.

Money language *was* carrying part of the effect, so R012-R014c were confounded by it and the claim
that the model had been shown no income was false. But a large gradient survives with no money cue
anywhere in the payload, and the census-only floor is still several times the real association. About
half the gradient is income information we supply and is removable; the rest rides on occupation,
education, tenure and housing size, and cannot be removed without removing the Census grounding the
project exists to provide.

Removal is not a remedy in either case, because rank agreement falls with it: `mean_spearman` goes
from 0.423 and 0.436 at baseline to 0.396 and 0.387 under R019a, and `share_top_match` from 0.528
and 0.486 to 0.431 and 0.375. The income information exaggerates the gradient and improves the
ranking at the same time.

Residual channels left in deliberately: occupation, education, tenure detail (mortgage against owned
free and clear), bedrooms, rooms, vehicles, state and region.

## 2. Response styles, fixed per respondent (R017b)

`assign_traits` was seeded on the per-repeat seed, so a persona was dealt a different style in every
repeat and only 22 of 100 kept theirs. It is now seeded on `seed_base`, with a test pinning it, and
all 100 keep their style.

The effect survives the fix but is narrower and more mixed than R017 reported. `share_top_match`
improves clearly — qwen 0.486 to 0.583, gemini 0.458 to 0.542 — and mean_tv improves for gemini and
mistral. But `mean_spearman` **falls** for gemini, 0.447 to 0.409, so styles do not improve rank
agreement for every model.

The repeat-agreement argument from R017, that agreement fell from 89% to 50% and showed a less
deterministic panel, is withdrawn. It was largely measuring the re-deal.

The income gradient also survives inside each style: 0.63 unstyled, 0.60 enthusiastic, 0.58
indifferent, 0.72 pragmatic, and 0.09 for sceptical only because sceptics answer at the bottom of the
scale regardless. Pooled it is 0.29. The lower pooled figure comes from mixing groups with different
levels, not from the model reading income differently — which is the mechanism as stated, but not
the stronger claim the earlier write-up implied.

## 3. The panel, pre-registered (R018b)

The mix and the panel were both written into `registry.csv` with result PENDING before the run, so
neither could be chosen after seeing the numbers. The panel was fixed as all four models styled,
dealt one per persona, 25 each — chosen on `share_top_match` from the previous round rather than on
mean_tv, with the style mix carried over unchanged rather than retuned.

It reaches `mean_spearman` **0.465** and `share_top_match` **0.556**, against 0.172 and 0.250 for the
random control and 0.423 and 0.436 for the two baseline models. `share_top_match` is the best of any
arm.

R018's post-hoc panel reported 0.533, so roughly 0.07 of that was selection: it had been picked from
161 combinations all scored against the same 100 real respondents. **0.465 is the number to quote.
0.533 should not be quoted again.** mean_tv 0.2488 is not quoted as evidence at all, because the
random control reaches 0.2117.

## 4. Withdrawn from the 2026-09-23 write-up

- **"39% reduction in distance"** — mean_tv, beaten by a random panel.
- **"Stop ablating the persona"** — premature; the ablation had not removed income.
- **"The scale endpoint gap is closed, slightly overshooting"** — that compared a synthetic rate
  pooled over 24 likert items against a real rate for Q1 alone. On matched items the panel
  undershoots. Only the Q1 level-5 rate is close.
- **"Q1 against Q2 substantially resolved"** — the panel's 16.7% is under a quarter of the real
  rate, which had not been computed when that was written.
- **Calibration on the real respondents** — a monotone transform preserves ranks, so it cannot change
  a rank correlation, and it would make the real 600 part of the generator.
- **HOA from Census** — ACS PUMS has no HOA variable. The housing columns are SERIALNO, PUMA, ADJINC,
  WGTP, NP, BDSP, BLD, RMSP, TEN, VEH, YRBLT, GRPIP, HHT, HINCP, MV, NOC, OCPIP.
- **Cost** — $27.14 from the manifests, not the $28.86 taken from log lines that double-counted
  repair rounds.

## Still open

- The income gradient is well above the real association in every arm, including the pre-registered
  panel. No intervention has closed it and removing the information makes agreement worse.
- Likert spread is still narrower than the real survey's in every arm.
- r(Q1, concept likelihood) is 0.9 or above even in styled panels: one early stance propagates
  through the concept block.
- Q24 HOA is far below the real rate everywhere, because no persona field carries HOA status and
  PUMS cannot supply one. Another source would be needed.
- The pre-registered panel has not been run on a held-out panel. S2 through S6 are untouched.

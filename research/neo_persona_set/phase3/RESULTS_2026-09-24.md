All arms: the same 100 personas (`out/phase1_interview_matched600_s1.csv`), all 39 items, two
repeats, temperature 0.2. Distances are mean total-variation over 34 of the 39 items, scored by

## What worked, and why

Two independent sources of between-respondent variance, and they compose.

**Response-style heterogeneity (R017).** Each persona is dealt a response style — sceptical 25%,
enthusiastic 20%, pragmatic 20%, indifferent 15%, and 20% left alone — by an RNG that never sees the
persona. On Gemini this moved every metric at once: distance 0.3297 to 0.3062, likert SD 0.82 to
1.08, and rho(income, Q1) from 0.75 to 0.30. On DeepSeek, 0.4026 to 0.3715, SD 0.62 to 0.75, rho
0.66 to 0.37.

This is the first thing to move the income gradient at all. Six persona ablations and five protocol
interventions failed at it. The mechanism is that the styles are orthogonal to income by
construction, so they add person-level variance that dilutes income's share of the total. Median
income by style runs 57k to 75k around a panel median of 69k at n=15-25, which is noise.

R005 as originally written — sceptical 30% plus pragmatic 20% — halved the gradient too (0.36 on
Gemini) but did not improve distance, because both of its styles lean negative and the whole
distribution shifted down. Adding the enthusiastic style is what turns the mechanism into an
improvement.

**Model-family heterogeneity (R018).** Each persona answered by one randomly assigned model. Four
unstyled models reach 0.2756; all four styled reach 0.2433; the best of all 162 combinations is
GPT-5-mini unstyled plus styled Gemini plus styled Mistral at 0.2360.

The combination is chosen against the real marginals, so it was checked by leaving one question out,
re-choosing the combination on the other 33 and scoring only the held-out one. That estimate is also
0.2360, and the same combination wins for all 34 held-out questions, so the selection is stable
rather than fitted. S2 through S6 were not touched.

## What failed, and why it is worth knowing

**Sampling from the model's own distribution (R016) does not work for chat models.** Jev's
probability vectors score 0.2901 where its argmax scores 0.4314, so for that architecture a third of
the distance was pure collapse. `--top-logprobs` recovers the same thing for chat models, and the
substitution moves DeepSeek 0.4044 to 0.4032 and Llama 4 Maverick 0.4538 to 0.4344 — almost nothing.

The reason, measured directly: DeepSeek puts 94.6% of its probability mass on the answer it gives
(0.17 bits of entropy) and Llama 90.8% (0.32 bits), against Jev's 53.6% and 1.53 bits. **Chat models
are not hiding a spread distribution behind a confident answer; they are simply confident.** That
retroactively explains why temperature (R003) and per-persona jitter (R009) both failed, and it means
spread has to be induced from outside the model — which is exactly what R017 and R018 do.

**Jev on the full instrument (R015) is the worst respondent we have**, 0.4330 by its answers. Its
apparent strength on Q1 in the three-question pilot did not survive asking all 39.

## Still open

- rho(income, Q1) is 0.35 against a real 0.10. Better than 0.66, not solved. Post-generation
  calibration (R014-family follow-up) remains the only untried route, and it needs the raw AYTM file
  for a train/test split.
- Likert SD is 1.01 against a real 1.30.
- r(Q1, concept likelihood) is still 0.9 or above: one early stance propagates through the concept
  block even in styled panels.
- Q24 HOA: real 21.3%, every model 1-6.5%. No persona field carries HOA status, so it is invented.
  Adding it from Census data is a cheap, obvious fix nobody has done yet.
- Logprobs are unavailable on Gemini and Mistral and rejected by Qwen's provider, so R016 can never
  be run on the three best-scoring models.

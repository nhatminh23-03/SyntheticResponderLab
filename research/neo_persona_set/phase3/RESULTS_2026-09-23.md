# S1 panel, 2026-09-23 — persona-information ablations and a cross-family comparison

All arms use the same 100 personas (`out/phase1_interview_matched600_s1.csv`, the matched draw),
the same 39-item instrument, two repeats, temperature 0.2, reasoning off, one call per persona.
30 runs, 6,000 respondent-instruments, $17.68 on OpenRouter.

Real-600 reference values: rho(income, Q1) ~ 0.10, Q1 shares 0.38 / 0.20 / 0.18 / 0.16 / 0.07,
likert SD ~ 1.3.

Distances to the real 600 are over 34 of the 39 items and come from
`phase3/score_vs_real_marginals.py`, which lifts the real marginals out of a comparison folder
`compare_real.py` wrote earlier rather than reading the AYTM file, which is deliberately not on this
machine. It reproduces compare_real's own per-question TV to within 0.0001 on Minh's baseline runs.
Q14, Q20 and Q22 need label normalisation that lives inside compare_real and are excluded.

## 1. Withholding financial information from the persona does not work

| condition | what was withheld | rho DeepSeek | rho Qwen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| baseline | nothing | 0.66 | 0.73 |
| R012 | exact_household_income (band kept) | 0.65 | 0.70 |
| R013 | the three money story sections | 0.62 | 0.71 |
| R014 | both of the above | 0.64 | 0.67 |
| R014b | + housing cost and its basis | 0.64 | 0.68 |
| R014c | + income_bucket, so no income at all | 0.62 | 0.52 |

Distance to the real 600 does not improve either — it drifts slightly the wrong way. DeepSeek runs
0.4026 at baseline and 0.4115 under R014c; Qwen runs 0.3533 and 0.3572.

Six conditions, and the largest movement is Qwen under R014c, which still sits five times above the
real association. The Q1 distribution, the top-2 share and the likert spread are unchanged
throughout. The prompt diffs were verified by `--dry-run` before the runs: exactly the named fields
left the payload and nothing else did.

The reason this fails is visible in the last row. A persona with no income field still carries an
occupation, an education level, a county, a bedroom count and a vehicle count, and the model reads
income off those. Grounding the persona in real Census microdata is the point of the project, so
that signal cannot be removed without removing the grounding.

The same pattern holds on the TypeSafe (Jev) classifier, run separately on the same panel: 0.72 at
baseline, 0.69 without the exact figure, 0.66 without the money story, 0.64 without both, and 0.49
only when every financial channel including the band was withheld.

**Conclusion.** The income-to-interest coupling is not something we are feeding the model. It is the
model's prior about who buys a $23,000 product. Persona design cannot reach it.

## 2. Changing the model family moves things that four protocol interventions could not

| model | origin | mean TV, 34 items | mean JS | Q1 TV | Q1 != Q2 | answers 4-5 (real ~23%) | rho(income, Q1) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| google/gemini-3-flash-preview | US | **0.3297** | **0.1476** | **0.270** | **20.0%** | 25.6% | 0.75 |
| mistralai/mistral-medium-3.1 | EU | 0.3535 | 0.1685 | 0.370 | 8.0% | **30.5%** | 0.74 |
| openai/gpt-5-mini | US | 0.3595 | 0.1721 | 0.415 | 21.0% | 27.2% | 0.71 |
| deepseek/deepseek-v4-pro-0813 | CN | 0.4026 | 0.2126 | 0.350 | 0.5% | 19.0% | 0.66 |
| qwen/qwen3.7-plus | CN | 0.3533 | 0.1667 | 0.365 | 0.0% | 13.7% | 0.73 |
| TypeSafe System One | US | — | — | — | 18.0% | 5.2% | 0.72 |

0.3297 is the lowest distance to the real 600 of any arm run on this panel to date. Scored the same
way, the best of Minh's protocol arms are Qwen at baseline (0.3482) and Qwen at temperature 0.8
(0.3527); DeepSeek's best is temperature 0.8 at 0.3826. Gemini beats both Chinese models on 17 of
the 34 questions, and it wins by much more than it loses: -0.305 on Q15, -0.218 on Q17 and -0.082
on Q1, against a worst loss of +0.215.

It also decouples Q1 from Q2 in one respondent in five. R007 (slicing the survey into four calls)
and R011 (a note telling the respondent that interest and likelihood are different questions) both
tried to break that lockstep through the protocol and both failed. Changing the model does it for
free.

The income gradient, by contrast, is flat across the whole table: six models, three countries and
two architectures all land between 0.52 and 0.75. Nothing about model choice touches it.

## 3. R008 as originally stated

The hypothesis was that American and European models differ on the HOA question and the permit
items. They do, and by much more than expected — but the split is not American against European.

| model | Q15 permit-light appeal, 4-5 | Q24 HOA = Yes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **real 600** | **44.8%** | **21.3%** |
| mistral-medium-3.1 (EU) | 69.0% | 6.5% |
| gemini-3-flash (US) | 40.0% | 6.0% |
| gpt-5-mini (US) | 23.0% | 1.5% |
| deepseek (CN) | 13.5% | 2.0% |
| qwen (CN) | 6.5% | 1.0% |

Q15 spans a factor of ten across models, and the real value sits near the top of that range. Gemini
lands almost on it (per-question TV 0.147, against 0.452 for DeepSeek and 0.507 for Qwen). Since
Q15 is one of the four items feeding RQ10 (best value driver), the answer to a client-facing
research question has until now depended more on which model we called than on the respondents, and
the two models we were using were the two furthest from the truth.

Q24 is a different problem and a shared one: the real 600 are 21.3% HOA and every model puts it
between 1% and 6.5%. No persona field carries HOA status, so each model is inventing it from a
prior, and every prior is wrong in the same direction. Q6 shows the same shape — cost is the top
concern for 63.5% of real respondents and for 99% of DeepSeek's.

`mistralai/mistral-large` could not hold the 39-item response format: 24% and 33% of respondents
unusable across the two repeats, fallback shares of 0.24 and 0.33, attention-check pass rates of
0.81 and 0.73. Both runs failed their guardrails and are excluded. It was replaced by
mistral-medium-3.1, which ran clean at zero fallback.

## 4. What is still wrong in every arm

- Likert spread is 0.60 to 0.82 against a real 1.3. Every model answers in a narrower band than
  people do.
- "Extremely interested" is 0.00 in all 6,000 synthetic instruments. In the real 600 it is 7%.
- r(Q1, concept likelihood) is 0.68 to 0.94: one early stance still propagates through the concept
  block, even in the models that decouple Q1 from Q2.

## Data provenance

Minh's folder (`6_subset_of_dataset_plus_the_all_600_persona_data`) is extracted outside the
repository, at `../SyntheticResponderLab-Assets/`. His `phase1_interview_matched600_s1.csv` is
byte-identical to the one these runs used, so every arm here and every arm of his is the same 100
people.

The AYTM raw-data CSV remains off this machine by design. The real marginals used above were lifted
from the comparison folder Minh's `compare_real.py` run produced on 2026-09-17; the scorer that
reads them reproduces his per-question distances to within 0.0001. The metrics that need
respondent-level joins into the real file — mean Spearman, the chi-square counts — are not
reproduced and still require a machine that holds the raw CSV.
